Ingredients We Dont Rte

We never use ingredients on this “NO” list. We believe in the precautionary path, which means if there isn’t clear proof that an ingredient is safe–we don’t use it. Safety first.

  • Chlorine Bleach Harmful or fatal if swallowed, can permanently damage skin, attacks membranes in your respiratory system, and doesn’t get rid of dirt or stains—it turns them white.

  • Ammonia Iritating and corrosive. Exposure can cause immediate burning of the nose, throat, and respiratory tract.

  • Abrasives Contain hard particles that can damage your sink and shower by scuffing their surfaces and giving dangerous bacteria a safe place to grow, breed, and multiply.

  • Phosphates Too much in our waterways leads to excessive algae growth, often resulting in the death of fish and aquatic plants.

  • Phthalates In the past few years, researchers have linked phthalates to asthma and breast cancer.

  • Triclosan Exposure to triclosan contributes to making bacteria resistant to antibiotics.

  • Parabens Known to disrupt hormone function.

  • Formaldehyde Can cause an allergic reaction in the skin; watery eyes; a burning sensation in the eyes, nose, and throat; coughing; wheezing; and nausea.

  • Quaternary Disinfectants Poisonous if ingested. They aren’t very effective on viruses, and their bacteria-killing power can be reduced by common conditions like encountering hard water.

Safe Rte

Naturally safe ingredients are our priority. The best naturally derived and plant-derived ingredients may cost a little more, but we take pride in investing in what matters most.

  • Fragrance A properly selected, safe fragrance brings a sense of clean to bathrooms and laundry and a sense of enjoyment to lotions and shampoos. We only select high-quality essential and fragrance oils that are guaranteed to meet our Safe Scent® standard, and meet the International Fragrance Association (IFRA) standards that protect against levels of irritation and sensitivity and must be free from contaminants. They undergo stringent testing for purity, quality, and all leave-on products undergo third-party sensitivity testing with a certified dermatologist. If it’s not safe, we won’t use it.

  • Colorants Color makes life better, enhances emotional mood, and makes our world more appealing. Color and safety go hand-in-hand. We use only safe colorants that are non-toxic and won’t accumulate in the environment. We select colors with the highest safety profiles for our home and personal care products. All are batch certified, have been thoroughly tested to be free of impurities and contaminants, and are Food, Drug & Cosmetic (FD&C) certified by the US Food & Drug Administration (FDA).

  • Preservatives Without proper preservatives, many natural products degrade quickly and can easily become home to mold and bacteria. The solution to keeping natural products fresh and safe is to add proven, safe, and effective preservatives. As of today, there are no effective plant-derived preservatives. If one were available, we would use it. In the meantime, we select the safest, most effective preservatives that allow us to use the tiniest amounts possible. We never use cheaper preservatives known to cause health issues such as formaldehyde or parabens.

Why chicken meal?

What is the difference between these ingredients?

In the pet food industry, chicken and chicken meal are both composed of the same thing: chicken meat, skin, and sometimes bone (which might sound unpleasant to you, but there’s no arguing that animals naturally love bones; they are highly nutritious and also support their connective tissues). There are two differences between these two very similar ingredients. The first difference is how they are processed and combined with other ingredients. The second—and more important—difference is the nutritional value.

Chicken meal Chicken
The ingredient “chicken meal” is ground chicken that has been dehydrated and rendered—a process that separates fat, removes water, kills any bacteria, viruses, parasites, and other organisms—creating a dried ingredient with a high concentration of nutrients. The ingredient “chicken” is raw, ground chicken that is frozen for shipping and handling to reduce the risk of food-borne illnesses.

Chicken meal and other desired ingredients are put into a machine that combines the mixture and produces the finished kibble or treat. Chicken is thawed, then put into a machine that combines it with the other ingredients, removes the excess moisture, renders it, and produces the finished kibble or treat.

This process essentially converts raw, ground chicken into chicken meal—but at a different point in the process.
Because chicken meal is a dry ingredient used to create a dry kibble or treat, it can be used in greater amounts without negatively affecting the final product. The high moisture content of chicken limits the total amount that can be used in the final product.
